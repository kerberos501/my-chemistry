# Visuals: lesson-by-lesson evaluation

Every lesson was read and judged on one question: would a picture or a working model help a student understand this page better than the text alone? The answer was yes for all 51, so each lesson gets a model on its right-hand page. This file records what each one is, why it is there, and whether it is built.

"Review deck" marks where a model was shaped by a common error or question from the course review decks.

**Status:** Parts 1, 2 and 3 are built (41 of 51 lessons). Part 4 is planned (10 lessons).

Every picture in the book is drawn for the book, in code, so each one shows exactly the example on its page and can be changed with it.

Every lesson also links to the matching section of OpenStax *Chemistry 2e*, a free online textbook, under “Read more”. Those are links only: nothing is copied from that book.

## Part 1. Counting atoms (built)

| Lesson | Model | What the student does | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Fixed recipes | Pull a sample apart | Changes the size of a sample; two jars fill in a fixed ratio | The ratio stays the same at every sample size |
| 1.2 Measured numbers | Figure counter and conversion track | Types a number to see which digits are significant; flips conversion factors until the units cancel | An upside-down factor shows up as units that will not cancel (review deck: inverted ratio) |
| 1.3 Names and formulas | Ion tiles | Adds cation and anion tiles, each as wide as its charge, until the rows match | Makes "charges add to zero" something you can see; shows where brackets go |
| 1.4 The mole | Same mass, different count | Puts the same mass of two substances in two pans; dots show moles | Equal masses do not hold equal numbers of particles |
| 1.5 Balanced equations | Atom counter | Steps coefficients up and down; molecules and an atom tally update | Coefficients change, formulas do not; the tally says which element is short |
| 1.6 Mass to mass | The mole bridge | Slides a starting mass across grams, moles, moles, grams | Only the middle span crosses substances; 1 g does not make 2 g |
| 1.7 What runs out first | Draining hoppers | Sets moles of each reactant and runs the reaction | The reactant with more moles can still be limiting (review deck: compare moles ÷ coefficient) |
| 1.8 Yield | Jar with a theoretical line | Sets mass burned and mass collected | Shows what over 100% looks like, and that yield works in both directions (review deck) |
| 1.9 Solutions | Dissolve, then dilute | Changes moles and volume; ions drawn as dots | Diluting keeps the dots and spreads them out; ion concentration depends on the formula (review deck) |
| 1.10 What dissolves | Solubility grid and mixer | Picks two solutions; the grid marks the new pairings | Turns the list of rules into a lookup students can test themselves against |
| 1.11 Ionic equations | Drifting ions | Mixes two solutions; reacting ions sink, spectators keep drifting and are crossed out | Shows why spectators cancel, and includes a mixture where nothing reacts |
| 1.12 Acids and bases | Titration | Adds base until the indicator turns, then calculates the unknown molarity | The equivalence point as an event; the 2 : 1 ratio for H₂SO₄ |
| 1.13 Redox | Follow the electrons, and a bookkeeping table | Runs a metal-in-solution reaction; steps through oxidation-number sums | Electrons lost equal electrons gained; oxidized means reducing agent (review deck) |

## Part 3. Bonds, shapes and solids (built)

| Lesson | Model | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 Why atoms bond | Pick two atoms; the shared pair drifts toward the stronger pull, or jumps | Bond type is a continuum set by the difference in electronegativity |
| 3.2 Lewis structures | The five steps, one at a time, with a counter of electrons left to place | The counting method step by step (review deck) |
| 3.3 Formal charge and resonance | Alternative drawings with a formal-charge table for every atom | Choosing the best structure; resonance as two equal drawings |
| 3.4 Breaking the octet rule | Central atoms with a meter of 4, 6, 7, 8 or 10 electrons | Which atoms can and cannot expand (review deck) |
| 3.5 Molecular shape | Choose domains and lone pairs; turn the molecule in three dimensions | Electron-domain geometry against molecular shape (review deck) |
| 3.6 Polar molecules | Bond-dipole arrows on a molecule you can turn, and their sum | Polar bonds do not always make a polar molecule; cis and trans (review deck) |
| 3.7 Hybrid orbitals | Domain count mapped to the orbitals that mix and the hybrids that result | Lone pairs count as domains (review deck) |
| 3.8 Sigma and pi bonds | Structures with σ bonds in black and π bonds in colour, and a tally | Every link has one σ; only multiple bonds have π (review deck) |
| 3.9 Bond enthalpy | Ledger of bonds broken and formed beside an energy path | Broken minus formed, and only the bonds that change (review deck) |
| 3.10 Forces between molecules | Pick a molecule; the forces it can have light up | Hydrogen bonding needs H on N, O or F: CH₃F is the trap (review deck) |
| 3.11 Boiling points | Boiling points of the hydrides of four groups | The jump for H₂O, NH₃ and HF; size against hydrogen bonding (review deck) |
| 3.12 Solids | Build a cubic cell from corner, face, body and edge atoms; the fractions are counted | The hardest picture in the course to hold in the head |
| 3.13 Density of a crystal | A slice through the cell showing the line where atoms touch, then the density worked out | Which diagonal goes with which cell (review deck) |
| 3.14 X-ray diffraction | Two layers of atoms and a beam you tilt until the extra path is a whole number of wavelengths | The sine doubles, not the angle (review deck) |
